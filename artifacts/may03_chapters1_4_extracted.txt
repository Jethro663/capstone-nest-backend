CHAPTER I  
INTRODUCTION 
The landscape of modern education is undergoing a massive digital transformation 
by transitioning from traditional classroom environments to highly augmented digital 
ecosystems. Central to this evolution is the Learning Management System which has 
become a  fundamental tool for educational institutions worldwide to facilitate lesson 
delivery, manage class rosters, and administer student assessments [1]. The basic 
architecture of a Learning Management System is heavily geared toward administrative 
control, st ructured content distribution, and rigorous compliance tracking [2]. In these 
environments, management and educators dictate exactly what content is delivered, the 
sequence of consumption, and the pacing of the overall course [3]. While the Learning 
Management System is exceptionally efficient at managing the logistical aspects of 
education and maintaining official academic records, the rigid top-down approach often fails 
to address the individualized cognitive needs of diverse student populations [4]. The global 
pandemic served as a catalyst for educational technology by forcing a rapid and often 
unplanned transition to remote and blended learning environments [1]. While this 
accelerated the adoption of basic Learning Management Systems including Moodle and  
Google Classroom to ensure instructional continuity and address skepticism toward online 
learning [2], it also exposed profound inequalities in how these systems handle diverse 
learning paces [3]. 
When institutions hastily implemented digital solutions, the focus was primarily on 
content delivery rather than cognitive scaffolding [1]. Consequently, students who lacked 
strong self-regulation skills or prerequisite knowledge found themselves rapidly falling 
behind and facing learning gaps that are exceptionally difficult to close once they occur [4]. 
The traditional Learning Management System is excellent at tracking completion but 
inherently lacks the diagnostic capability to identify the specific roo t causes of a student 
misunderstanding, which often renders standard online learning isolating for struggling 
learners [5].

For these students, the traditional Learning Management System often functions 
merely as a repository of static information rather than an active tool for academic recovery, 
leading to disengagement and compounded learning loss. 
To address the inherent limitations of the traditional Learning Management System, 
the educational technology sector has seen the rise of the Learning Experience Platform. 
Unlike a Learning Management System, a Learning Experience Platform operates on a 
bottom-up learner centric paradigm that emphasizes extreme personalization, self-directed 
learning, and continuous skill growth [ 5]. A Learning Experience Platform utilizes advanced 
algorithms to curate diverse multi format digital resources including microlearning modules, 
podcasts, and interactive videos, tailoring the educational journey to a student specific goals 
and historical engagement patterns [2]. Research indicates that organizations and 
institutions deploying personalized experience driven platfor ms achieve thirty-five to fifty 
percent higher course completion rates and up to sixty percent greater learner engagement 
compared to traditional compliance focused Learning Management System environments 
[3]. Experimental studies further validate that tar geted behavioral interventions through a 
Learning Experience Platform can extend daily average learning durations by forty-one-point 
five percent and significantly increase voluntary reading volumes, which positively correlates 
with overall academic performance [4]. 
The integration of Artificial Intelligence into these educational platforms represents 
a profound leap in capability, effectively bridging the gap between mass instruction and 
individualized support. Artificial Intelligence driven systems provide predictiv e analytics, 
enabling educators to anticipate academic risks early and deliver just in time support without 
disrupting core classroom instruction [1]. One of the most significant breakthroughs is the 
application of Large Language Models and Natural Languag e Processing to automate the 
generation of instructional materials. Traditional Natural Language Processing methods for 
extracting concepts from educational documents required resource intensive manual labor 
[3]. However, modern Large Language Models demonstrate advanced reasoning 
capabilities, utilizing techniques including Named Entity Recognition and Relation Extraction 
to automatically parse unstructured text including standard PDF modules into structured 
machine-readable instructional data [3].

Beyond content generation, Artificial Intelligence serves as an active pedagogical

System functions as a continuous twenty-four seven learning companion that adapts in real

challenge in secondary education, with interactive, persona-driven AI serving as a powerful 
mechanism to rebuild motivation  [3]. Studies on the "Uncanny Valley" hypothesis in 
education reveal that a moderate degree of anthropomorphism in AI tutors enhances 
emotional engagement and generates high levels of learner trust, provided the AI is carefully 
designed to balance relatability with clear artificiality to avoid psychological discomfort [4].  
The pedagogical logic for deploying these AI interventions relies heavily on Mastery 
Learning, an instructional philosophy positing that students must achieve a high level of 
competence in prerequisite knowledge before advancing [1]. Digital systems operationalize 
this through algorithms like Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT), which estimate a student's 
knowledge state and enforce mastery criteria  [2]. While highly technical subjects may 
demand a 95 percent mastery threshold, practical applications in genera lized secondary 
education frequently employ a  74% threshold to differentiate between basic comprehension 
and critical learning gaps  [3]. Gating access to remedial interventions based on this 
threshold ensures that intensive, AI-driven support is aggressively targeted toward students 
who are fundamentally failing, preventing high-performing students from being subjected to 
redundant exercises [1].

features, personalized AI mentoring, and dynamic threshold-based remediation required to 
actively intervene when a student fails to comprehend an e-SLM [13].  
Despite these technological advancements, a significant implementation gap 
currently exists in Philippine public secondary education. A comprehensive report by the 
ASEAN Foundation indicates that 83 percent of Filipino students actively utilize generative AI 
tools for educational purposes, yet only 42 percent of educators use these tools for their own 
tasks [1]. This profound generational and technological divide means that student AI usage 
is frequently driven by convenience rather than a strong technical understanding of 
responsible use  [8]. Furthermore, students are integrating these technologies faster than 
curricula can adapt, leading to uncritical use and challenges for metacognitive engagement.

schools without unified frameworks to harness AI safely and effectively [12].  
 At Gat Andres Bonifacio High School, the challenges of delivering timely academic

1.1 PROJECT CONTEXT 
The persistent challenge in modern secondary education is not merely the delivery of 
instruction but the equitable provision of timely and individualized remediation for students 
who fail to grasp core competencies. While Learning Management Systems have been widely 
adopted to streamline the distribution of curriculum and manage administrative workflows, 
a critical review of existing literature reveals a profound gap in their capacity to support 
personalized academic interventions [1]. Traditional Learning Management System 
platforms operate as static repositories because they excel at compliance tracking and 
grade logging but inherently lack the agile and adaptive mechanisms required to diagnose 
granular learning gaps and autonomously deploy customized recovery paths. Consequently, 
when a student struggles, the burden of intervention falls entirely on the classroom teacher 
who must manually analyze the data, curate alternative materials, and monitor the student 
progress offline. This process is highly unsust ainable in public school environments where 
student-to-teacher ratios are high and resources are limited. 
This technological gap is starkly evident within the Philippine K -12 educational 
landscape. In response to severe learning losses, DepEd introduced national remediation 
initiatives such as "Catch-Up Fridays," designed specifically to address foundational deficits

integrated web and mobile -based Learning Management System featuring 
embedded Learning Experience Platform components, specifically engineered to 
automate and deliver targeted academic intervention for high school students at Gat

distributions, and standardized assessments, while simultaneously providing  an 
autonomous, personalized remediation environment for students who demonstrate 
quantifiable learning difficulties. 
What makes Nexora unique and highly relevant is its architectural solution to 
the critical gaps in public education: the seamless bridging of rigid curriculum 
compliance with agile, AI -driven intervention. The platform operates on a dynamic 
gating mechanism. Access to the highly engaging Learning Experience Platform (LXP)

1.3 OBJECTIVES 
General Objective 
To design and develop Nexora, a web and mobile -based Learning 
Management System integrated  with a Learning Experience Platform that supports 
all subjects and grade levels at Gat Andres  Bonifacio High School, providing 
automated, targeted learning interventions for selected students, alleviating teacher 
workload through AI-driven content extraction. 
Specific Objectives  
1 To develop a comprehensive LMS that enables teachers and administrators to 
manage classes, students, and assessments, featuring the integration of Large 
Language Models (LLMs) and Natural Language Processing (NLP). 
2 To integrate an LXP within the LMS that acts as a targeted intervention 
environment, dynamically restricting access only to students demonstrating 
learning gaps by enforcing a strict 74% mastery threshold. 
3 To design and implement an anthropomorphic AI Non -Player Character (NPC) 
mentor functioning as an Intelligent Tutoring System (ITS) that assists struggling 
students by explaining mistakes, providing adaptive hints, and recommending 
remedial activities, compensating for the lack of specialized remedial educators. 
4 To implement automated performance tracking and data analytics within the LMS 
to provide teachers with real-time insights into student competency gaps. 
5 To evaluate the proposed system based on standard software metrics 
including functionality, reliability, usability, and portability . 
6 To establish and maintain strict data security, ethical AI usage guidelines, and 
privacy protocols within the platform to address the lack of institutional AI 
frameworks and protect sensitive student academic records.

1.4 SCOPE AND DELIMITATION 
SCOPE 
• The Nexora system is designed to support Grades 7, 8, 9, and 10 and subjects 
at Gat Andres Bonifacio High School through a responsive web and mobile -
accessible application. 
• The system features core Learning Management System (LMS) 
functionalities, allowing teachers to create / upload lesson materials, 
administer assessments, and manage student rosters.  
• The platform includes a built-in Learning Experience Platform (LXP) designed 
exclusively for targeted academic intervention.  
• Access to the LXP environment is dynamically gated; it is accessible only to 
students who score below a defined 74% performance threshold on their LMS 
assessments. 
• The system  incorporates Artificial  Intelligence (AI) and  Large Language 
Models (LLMs) to automatically extract text and generate structured 
instructional formulas, key points, and  quizzes from uploaded  PDF learning 
modules using Named Entity Recognition (NER) and Relation Extraction (RE). 
• An AI Non-Player Character (NPC) mentor (Intelligent Tutoring System) is 
integrated into the  LXP to interact with struggling students, explain errors, 
provide hints, and guide them through remedial paths. 
• Teachers are provided with an administrative dashboard within the LMS to 
seamlessly monitor, track, and override LXP activities and student 
intervention progress. 
• A comprehensive system evaluation will be conducted assessing 
functionality, reliability, usability, and portability based on user feedback 
from selected students and faculty. 
DELIMITATION 
• The system is designed to augment, not replace, traditional classroom 
instruction, teacher decision-making, or face-to-face pedagogy. 
• The platform excludes the full automation of official DepEd grading systems

CHAPTER 2 
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND SYSTEMS 
This chapter provides a critical overview of previous research, empirical studies, and 
existing technological frameworks relevant to the development of integrated educational 
platforms. The review conveys the established knowledge concerning Learning Management 
Systems (LMS), Learning Experience Platforms (LXP), the integration of Artificial Intelligence 
(AI) in education, and the pedagogical principles of targeted academic interventions. By 
critically discussing both foreign and local literature, this chapter establishes the theoretical 
and technological framework for the present study. Furthermore, a detailed comparative 
analysis of related systems highlights current technological gaps, providing robust 
justification for the development of Nexora. This int egrated Learning Management System 
and Learning Experience Platform is designed to facilitate automated and targeted 
remediation assisted by Artificial Intelligence for public high school students at Gat Andres 
Bonifacio High School. 
2.1 RELATED LITERATURE/STUDIES 
The transition from traditional, analog classroom instruction to digitally augmented 
pedagogical environments has necessitated a critical evaluation of how educational 
technology serves diverse learner profiles. The following subsections synthesize global and 
local studies to construct a comprehensive understanding of the technological mechanisms

The Evolution and Paradigms of Learning Management Systems (LMS)

hierarchical content distribution, and strict compliance tracking [1]. Within this framework, 
management and educational administrators determine precisely what content is delivered,

Learning Experience Platforms (LXP): Learner-Centric Paradigms and Engagement 
In response to the limitations of traditional Learning Management System (LMS)  
architectures, the Learning Experience Platform (LXP) emerged as a paradigm shift toward 
learner-centric, self-directed education. Unlike the closed syllabus approach of a Learning 
Management System (LMS), a Learning Experience Platform (LXP) operates on principles of 
extreme personalization, utilizing artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms to 
curate and recommend multi -format resources tailored to a learner's sp ecific goals, skill 
gaps, and historical engagement patterns [4]. The pedagogical philosophy underpinning the 
Learning Experience Platform (LXP)  is the facilitation of autonomous learning. Learning 
Experience Platform (LXP)  fosters this autonomy by incorporating microlearning, social 
collaboration, and gamification, thereby shifting the locus of control from the system 
administrator directly to the learner [1]. By analyzing data on learners' interactions, Learning 
Experience Platform (LXP)  provides a flexible approach to education that mimics popular 
content consumption algorithms, actively suggesting relevant videos, podcasts, articles, 
and interactive modules [9].

Mastery Learning Frameworks and Performance Thresholds in Remedial Education 
The pedagogical foundation for gating access to remedial Learning Experience 
Platform environments within a blended system relies entirely on the principles of Mastery 
Learning. Originally introduced in the 1960s by educational psychologists, mastery learn ing 
posits that students must achieve a predefined, high level of competence in prerequisite 
knowledge before they are permitted to advance to subsequent, more complex material [1]. 
Unlike traditional, time -based educational models that push entire cohorts  forward 
regardless of individual comprehension, mastery models utilize continuous formative 
assessments to identify specific competency levels [2]. If a student fails to meet the required 
threshold, they do not advance. Instead, they are diverted into cor rective activities, peer 
tutoring, small group discussions, or specialized digital remediation until mastery is 
unequivocally demonstrated [3].  
Mastery learning has been empirically proven to be a highly powerful instructional 
method, yielding moderate to large effect sizes ranging from 0.50 to 0.58 across all levels of 
education [1]. Determining the precise threshold for mastery, however, is a su bject of 
extensive educational data mining research. Systems employing Bayesian Knowledge 
Tracing, which is a standard algorithm used in intelligent tutoring systems to model student 
knowledge, frequently analyze student performance to set binary indicators of mastery [2]. 
While some highly rigorous technical subjects demand a 90 to 95 percent mastery 
criterion, and recent Bayesian Knowledge Tracing ( BKT) research suggests that pushing 
thresholds to 0.98 can yield additional benefits for future learning retention, [1 1] practical 
implementations in generalized secondary education frequently utilize a 74 percent 
threshold to differentiate between basic comprehension and critical learning gaps. [ 4] 
Research highlights that in large public education systems, particularly in the United States 
and developing nations, approximately 40 to 60 percent of first-year college students require 
some form of remediation in core subjects like mathematics and reading [3].

2.3 SYNTHESIS 
The review of literature and related systems underscores a critical paradigm shift in 
educational technology: the necessary transition from passive content management 
systems to active, personalized learning intervention platforms. While traditional Learning 
Management Systems (LMS) have successfully digitized administrative tasks, course 
scheduling, and formal content delivery, they inherently fail to address the individualized 
cognitive needs of struggling learners. To support these students using a traditional Learning 
Management System (LMS) requires, unsustainable manual input from educators who must 
diagnose gaps, create alternative materials, and monitor individual progress offline. 
Conversely, isolated Learning Experience Platforms (LXPs) and intelligent Artificial 
Intelligence (AI) tutoring applications provide excellent engagement, adaptive pacing, and 
personalization, but they often operate completely outside the rigorous, structured 
curriculum management and grading frameworks required by formal public educ ational 
institutions. The primary justification for the present study lies in the unaddressed 
technological intersection of these platforms, particularly within the localized, highly 
constrained context of Philippine public secondary education.  
At Gat Andres Bonifacio High School, educators manage exceptionally large class 
sizes and face severe instructional time constraints, making the manual delivery of 
individualized remedial instruction nearly impossible. National educational initiatives, such 
as DepEd’s Catch-Up Fridays, highlight a desperate, systemic need for remediation in core 
competencies like reading and mathematics. However, current literature explicitly proves 
that these efforts are bottlenecked by a lack of specialized teachers, insu fficient targeted 
learning materials, and extreme educator fatigue. Furthermore, while the existing DepEd 
DLMS is highly scalable and effective for module distribution, it simply does not possess the 
technological architecture to autonomously support these  remedial initiatives or harness

Pedagogical and Contextual Requirements 
Beyond technical metrics, Nexora must adhere to specific pedagogical requirements 
dictated by the Philippine educational landscape. Although internet connectivity is a 
documented challenge in public schools, the platform is designed to operate efficiently

JAKIPIR mentor, creating a "psychologically safe" environment that fosters learner trust and 
motivation without causing dissonance.
